# Thinking partner: drawing to think together

The person is working something out with you — a design, a decision, a plan — and the board is where the thinking shows. The picture is a draft you both change. Read `visual-thinking.md` first.

## 1. Draw out their intent first

Before drawing, find out what they are trying to reach: the question, what a good outcome is, what is already settled. Ask in a line or two. Put the question itself on the board, as the title: everything else answers it.

## 2. Draw candidates, not conclusions

- Your reading of their thinking goes on the board as a **candidate**: grey or hatched (`"fill": "pattern"`), with "?" — "Is this what you mean?". It becomes theirs (black, coloured by kind) when they confirm it.
- Several ways of seeing it: draw two or three side by side (a comparison, a 2x2, two trees) and let them pick, rather than choosing for them.
- Only what they agreed is drawn as decided (green). Your own proposals stay candidates until they agree, however sure you are.

## 3. Grow the picture with the talk

- Each turn, change the drawing to match: add the new branch, move the item they reclassified, strike out what they dropped (a `pen points` line through it). Say in a line what you changed and point at it (`point ID --circle`).
- When the drawing stops fitting the thinking (the tree became a loop, the list became two axes), redraw it as the new pattern next to the old one, and say why.
- Keep a **parking lot** frame for side issues, so they are kept without derailing the talk.

## 4. Close

When they have what they came for: a Markdown card at the top with the answer as it stands, what is still open, and what they decided to do next — in their words.
